📄 PAPER 2: The Micro Side-Channel Study
Proposed Title: Blindfolded Quality: Inferring Video Streaming Resolution and QoE from Fully Encrypted QUIC Traffic at the ISP Edge
Target Venues: IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management (TNSM), Computers & Security, USENIX Security.
Research Focus: Cybersecurity, Side-Channel Analysis, Privacy Inference.

Abstract / Executive Summary
To protect user privacy against passive surveillance, major content delivery networks have adopted Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) and QUIC, successfully obfuscating both the payload and the Server Name Indication (SNI). However, physical layer dynamics—specifically packet sizes and variable-bitrate (VBR) chunking patterns—cannot be entirely encrypted. In this paper, we investigate the vulnerability of fully encrypted QUIC flows to side-channel QoE (Quality of Experience) inference in a live ISP environment. Using a 7-day capture of residential traffic multiplexed through a Carrier-Grade NAT (CGNAT), we apply unsupervised clustering models to flow-level metadata (packet size distributions, inter-arrival times, and burst rates). We demonstrate that even when SNI and payloads are completely hidden, the underlying physics of video streaming leak distinct signatures, allowing ISPs to passively classify video resolution (e.g., 4K vs. 1080p) and user QoE with high accuracy.

Core Research Questions
To what extent do fully encrypted QUIC-based video streaming flows retain distinct packet-size and burst-rate signatures corresponding to video resolution?

Can unsupervised statistical clustering accurately differentiate between high-throughput video streaming (e.g., Netflix/YouTube 4K) and generic encrypted web browsing using only metadata?

How severely does the multiplexing behavior of a residential CGNAT degrade the accuracy of this passive side-channel inference?

Methodology & Dataset
Source: The exact same Zenodo dataset generated for Paper 1.

Feature Engineering: Extracting flow duration, packet size histograms, byte-per-packet ratios, and TCP/UDP inter-arrival times from the Zeek conn.log.

Machine Learning / Statistics: Applying Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) or clustering (K-Means/DBSCAN) to group flows strictly by their "shape."

Validation: Correlating the detected "heavy streaming" clusters with the peak diurnal hours (8:00 PM – 11:00 PM) established in Paper 1.

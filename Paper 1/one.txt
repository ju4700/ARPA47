📄 PAPER 1: The Macro Measurement Study
Proposed Title: Going Dark at the Edge: A 7-Day Empirical Measurement of QUIC and Encrypted Client Hello in a South Asian ISP
Target Venues: PAM (Passive and Active Measurement), ACM IMC, Elsevier Computer Networks.
Research Focus: Network Engineering, Protocol Evolution, ISP Traffic Management.

Abstract / Executive Summary
The widespread deployment of HTTP/3 (QUIC) and Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) fundamentally alters the visibility ISPs rely on for traffic engineering and Quality of Service (QoS). While previous measurements of ECH adoption have largely focused on European or North American transit networks and active scans, there is a severe lack of empirical data detailing how this protocol shift manifests at the residential edge in emerging markets. This study presents a 7-day passive measurement of 4,000 residential subscribers behind a Carrier-Grade NAT (CGNAT) in South Asia. Utilizing a bifurcated pipeline of macroscopic IPFIX telemetry and microscopic TLS metadata extraction (via Zeek), we quantify the real-world adoption rate of ECH and QUIC. We demonstrate that the migration to UDP-based encrypted transport heavily disrupts legacy traffic classification, leaving ISP active queue management operating blindly during peak diurnal shifts.

Core Research Questions
What is the current volumetric split between legacy HTTPS (TCP/TLS 1.2+) and QUIC (UDP) in a residential South Asian ISP?

What percentage of TLS 1.3 Client Hellos successfully utilize ECH, and which Autonomous Systems (e.g., Cloudflare, Meta, Google) are driving this adoption?

How does the dominance of UDP-based QUIC impact connection state tracking within standard ISP CGNAT architecture?

Methodology & Dataset
Source: Real-world traffic from 4,000 ISP clients (Bangladesh).

Macroscopic View: IPFIX (NetFlow v10) capturing flow volumes and diurnal TCP vs. UDP ratios for all 4,000 clients.

Microscopic View: TZSP port-mirroring of a /24 CGNAT subnet (225 clients) processed by Zeek to extract ssl.log (SNI, ECH flags, TLS versions) and conn.log metadata.

Privacy: Complete payload truncation; all IP addresses cryptographically hashed to ensure absolute anonymity.
